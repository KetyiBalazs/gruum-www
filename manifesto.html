Gruum Manifesto

Every product eventually drifts from itself. It used to happen slowly — a button copied from one Photoshop file to the next, nudged slightly by whoever touched it last, until nobody remembered which version was actually right. Design systems were supposed to fix this: tokens, styles, variables, components — a shared vocabulary instead of a folder of copies. And they helped. Products got more consistent, teams got faster. But most of them stopped at the surface, and none of them were ever truly shared — every team still keeps its own source of truth, in its own tool, in its own format, and now AI can build and ship faster than any one of those systems can keep up with.

Design was never just the visuals. It’s the color and the type, yes — but it’s also how a component behaves, how a flow reads, how an interaction responds when someone pushes on it. A system that only governs swatches and components in a Figma file is documenting half the product and leaving the rest to drift. Speed shouldn’t cost you that integrity, in any of its layers. That’s the trade we refuse to make you choose. Left unwatched, small inconsistencies compound — a color that doesn’t quite match, a component that quietly forked, a behavior nobody meant to change — until the product looks and feels stitched together by five different teams, because it was.

Gruum is the real design system: not a style guide, not a plugin bolted onto Figma, but the actual layer underneath everything else. It stores the source of truth, governs how it’s used, and corrects it the moment something drifts — across every tool your team touches, and every visual, behavioral, and language decision that makes the product feel like one thing. And because AI now builds as much of your product as your team does, Gruum is built AI-ready from the start: a design system your coding agents can actually read, follow, and be held to — not documentation they never open. Build on Gruum: the stable foundation that keeps your product aligned, no matter how fast you build, or who’s building it.
